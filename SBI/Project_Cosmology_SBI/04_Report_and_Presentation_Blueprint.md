## PART C — Project-specific viva questions (rehearse these)

The Q&A can cover the project *and* any of the 12 chapters. Chapter Q&A is in each `Chapter_xx/02_Viva_Questions_Bangla.md`. Here are **project-specific** ones with crisp answers.

**Q. Why is this a simulation-based / likelihood-free problem?**
→ We have a simulator (CAMB+noise) easy to sample from; we learn the posterior directly from simulations via amortized NPE instead of evaluating an explicit likelihood. (Even though noise is Gaussian, the workflow generalizes to intractable likelihoods.)

**Q. What does "amortized" mean here?**
→ We pay a big one-time training cost; afterwards inference for *any* new spectrum is a fast forward pass — no re-running MCMC per dataset.

**Q. Why log + standardize `P(k)`?**
→ Raw `P(k)` spans orders of magnitude; log compresses dynamic range; standardization gives the network well-scaled inputs → stable training.

**Q. Why sample `ln(10¹⁰Aₛ)` not `Aₛ`?**
→ Aₛ is tiny and spans orders of magnitude; the log makes the prior well-scaled and the amplitude enters ~linearly in log-space. (A change-of-variables, Ch1.)

**Q. ⭐ Why is H₀ poorly constrained / degenerate with Ωₘ?**
→ In h-units (k in h/Mpc, P in (Mpc/h)³) the linear spectrum's shape depends on physical densities Ωₘh², Ω_b h² — not H₀ alone. So H₀ is weakly constrained and degenerate with Ωₘ along constant Ωₘh². The turnover k_eq ∝ Ωₘh² is the main handle.

**Q. How do you know the posterior is trustworthy?**
→ SBC (uniform ranks / ECDF in band) shows calibration; posterior contraction & z-scores show informativeness & unbiasedness; coverage matches nominal.

**Q. A wide H₀ posterior — is that a failure?**
→ No. A calibrated, appropriately wide posterior is the correct scientific result given the data's limited H₀ information. Calibration matters more than tightness.

**Q. What's your "real data"? Is it really real?**
→ A Planck-2018 fiducial spectrum + noise (known ground truth) — a controlled real-world-like test. True survey data would need galaxy bias, RSD, nonlinearities, survey window (stated as a limitation).

**Q. Why a summary network, and why not a DeepSet?**
→ To compress the 100-point spectrum to a low-dim embedding learned jointly with the flow. Not a DeepSet because the spectrum is an *ordered* curve over k — order (turnover, BAO) is informative; permutation-invariance would destroy it.

**Q. What is the noise model and where does √(2/N) come from?**
→ Cosmic variance: each k-bin averages N_i Fourier modes; the band power has relative error √(2/N_i) (Gaussian approx to a χ² with N_i modes). Few modes at low k → noisy; many at high k → precise.

**Q. How does this connect to the course?**
→ Prior sampling (Ch3), Monte Carlo posterior summaries (Ch1), posterior predictive checks (Ch4), SBC (Ch9), likelihood-free inference (Ch10), neural networks (Ch11), NPE/BayesFlow (Ch12).
